\begin{abstract}
We report three anomalies observed in preregistered experiments where populations of
LLM-driven or scripted agents play a shared political-economy game under a deterministic
Rust engine with bit-exact replay. First, a \emph{protocol-compliance cliff}: in a single-model case study, a frontier
instruction-tuned model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) fails to copy a menu object on 0 of 100
first mandatory moves when the legal menu contains a single action, while complying on
richer menus---compliance is a property of menu poverty, not of prompting alone. Second, a
\emph{volume-confounded concentration metric}: comparing recipient concentration across
agent populations with different activity levels reverses the sign of the signal (a
targeting attacker appears less concentrating than a uniform null on 18 of 20 paired
seeds) while a conditional permutation test flags the same games at $p<0.05$ on every
seed. Third, an \emph{adversary-coverage bound on a mechanism}: exhaustive enumeration of
the influential subspace of legal attack policies (42 genomes) keeps the
largest-recipient-share cap at $0.07$ against a preregistered $0.08$ threshold (versus
$0.806$ under the unbalanced interface), but breaches the recipient-HHI line
($0.0302>0.03$) on one of five held-out seeds through a previously unnoticed action
combination; the preregistered downgrade fires. Every number in this note is recomputed
from published data rows by a verification script that runs green in public CI, and the
verifier itself is mutation-hardened: ten controlled corruptions of the data, including
one that regenerates file hashes, are all caught.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

The ALASHI world places 100 agents in one six-round political-economy game: production,
markets, bribes, law votes and vetoes, with actions admitted only from a bounded menu of
legal options probed from a canonical Rust rule engine. Every decision is journaled with
its exact prompt and menu; every game is verified by exact replay against the same engine
binary. The public package \texttt{world-100-grid} contains 50 recorded games (7{,}410
bribe decisions) across five arms: a uniform-legal null (20 seeds), a hand-designed legal
attacker \emph{L4} that bribes the richest visible legal target (21 games), a positional
control (3), an ablation that hides opponent wealth in the observation (3), and one
stopped model-driven attempt. The two recorded BitLLM games that motivated the mechanism
fix (largest recipient share $80.56\%\to 7.14\%$ after interface balancing) are cited from
the source repository and predate the package.

Three preregistered decision rules fixed thresholds before data (documents
\texttt{20261113-bribe-grid-prereg}, \texttt{20261113-blind-ablation-prereg},
\texttt{20261114-prosecutor-pass} in the engine repository), and every headline number
below is recomputed by \texttt{verify.py} from the published parquet rows; a green run of
that script is the acceptance test of the package and runs in GitHub CI on every push.

\section{Anomaly 1: the protocol-compliance cliff}

\textbf{Setup.} The world driver asks every agent one decision per phase. The first
mandatory decision of a game is a round-one market phase in which the engine admits
exactly one legal action, \texttt{pass}; the model must return one JSON object copied
from \texttt{allowed\_actions}. We ran Meta Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (NVIDIA NIM serving,
temperature 0) against this protocol with both the stock system prompt and a
format-anchored variant that explicitly forbids inventing actions.

\textbf{Result.} Table~\ref{tab:compliance} shows the cliff across families. Llama-3.1-8B
(NVIDIA NIM serving, bf16) answered an invented action on 0-of-100 first moves and
stopped at the preregistered threshold; Mistral-7B (Ollama, Q4) invents a bribe with a
nonexistent parameter name on the same poor menu (3/3 stable runs). Gemma-2-9B and
Phi-3.5 (Ollama, Q4) copy the exact menu object on every run. On the richer menu, all
four families return exact menu objects. Serving quantization differs across rows
(NIM bf16 versus Ollama Q4) and each Ollama verdict was confirmed stable over three
temperature-0 runs; the harness and both recorded prompts ship in the dataset package
so any family can be added with one command.

\textbf{Implication.} Protocol-compliance smoke tests on the \emph{poorest} legal menu
are a one-call, zero-cost gate that should precede any benchmark spend on a
bounded-action protocol. The full failure record (prompts, answers, menu sizes) ships in
the package.

\section{Anomaly 2: volume-confounded concentration}

\textbf{Setup.} We compare the L4 attacker against the uniform-legal null on 20 paired
seeds, measuring (i) recipient concentration (largest share, HHI) of accepted bribes,
(ii) the within-offer wealth percentile of chosen targets against engine-truth cash
(midrank), and (iii) a conditional permutation null that re-picks targets uniformly
within each recorded offer set ($B{=}2000$).

\textbf{Result.} The targeting signal is total: the attacker's chosen targets sit
$+0.244$ above the null in midrank percentile (95\% bootstrap CI $[0.236, 0.276]$;
positive on 20/20 seeds; percentile bootstrap over seeds, seeded RNG,
policy stated in the repository; visible and null arms' ranges do not overlap). Yet
the attacker's raw HHI is \emph{lower} than the null's on 18 of 20 seeds, because it
bribes about four times more often and concentration shrinks mechanically with volume.
The conditional permutation test (one-sided by preregistration) flags every L4
game at $p<0.05$ on both share and HHI; the Wilcoxon signed-rank test on paired
seeds agrees with the sign test throughout.

\textbf{Implication.} Between-arm concentration comparisons without volume or offer-set
conditioning invert the sign of the effect. The correct detector for legal targeting
under a bounded menu is the conditional null on recorded offers; the paired-delta table
ships as a case artifact in the silent-but-valid corpus.

\section{Anomaly 3: what exhaustive adversary search does to a mechanism claim}

\textbf{Setup.} A discrete policy space of 7{,}938 genomes (targeting rule, bribe
appetite, sell rule, alternate action, law vote, stickiness) is screened
one-factor-at-a-time around the L4 anchor on three training seeds (influence threshold
$0.005$). Three genes are influential---the alternate action (\texttt{donkey} instead of
\texttt{produce}: $+0.0180$ on mean share, the largest single effect), the sell rule, and
partially the targeting rule; appetite, law vote and herding stickiness fall below
threshold. The influential subspace (42 genomes) is enumerated exhaustively; the top five
plus anchors plus a uniform reference run on five held-out seeds disjoint from all
published seeds.

\textbf{Result.} The share cap held everywhere: worst $0.0700$ against the preregistered
$0.08$ (and against $0.8056$ under the original unbalanced interface). The HHI line did
not: the genome \emph{richest-targeting, slow selling, donkey} reached $0.0302$ against
the $0.03$ threshold on held-out seed 20261303. A cross-path check through the full
production driver (HTTP serving, journaling) reproduced the harness numbers exactly
(100 bribes, 46 recipients, share $0.07$, HHI $0.0302$).

\textbf{Implication.} The preregistered kill fired (the 0.7\% relative overshoot on one of five
seeds detects the edge of the mechanism, it does not locate it): the transfer
claim \emph{constraint
beats detection} downgrades to \emph{the bounded menu holds against the enumerated policy
families}. The breach is marginal ($0.0302$ vs $0.03$; one seed of five) and was found by
enumeration, not by evolutionary search; both facts are part of the record. The uncovered
\texttt{donkey} effect (an economic-rhythm change that concentrates bribes) is a concrete,
decomposable target for mechanism response.

\section{Verification: the claims are the artifact}

\texttt{verify.py} recomputes 24 checks (claims A--G) from the published rows: exact
acceptance-run counts (224 bribes / 70 recipients / share $8/224$ / HHI $0.02132494$),
null quantiles, positional-control ranges, conditional breaches of every L4 game, the
blind arm's dead aiming (midrank $0.49$--$0.50$ against engine cash), the $20/20$ paired
effect, the $18/20$ negative HHI deltas, and the model stop. The verifier was itself
attacked: a mutation harness corrupts the parquet in ten controlled ways; after hardening
(split hashing plus per-game agreement checks), all ten are caught, including a
sophisticated corruption that regenerates file hashes and is caught by the logic layer
alone. An independent reimplementation (Python, from raw journaled calls, bypassing the
export pipeline) reproduces the three headline statistics bit-exactly.

\section{Scope}

This is a mechanism study with machine-verified claims on one engine and one
game family; external generality across environments (for example, rank
correlation against fixed-rule arenas) is untested and is the natural follow-up.
The compliance cliff is tested across four families (two fail); the venue norm
for compliance claims is five to nine families, and the public one-command harness
lets third parties extend the table.

\section{Limitations}

Single-author study; seeds are finite (20 paired, 5 held-out) and held-out seeds were
chosen as a disjoint block, not sampled; the model datapoint is one model version under
one serving stack; single-gene screening may hide interactions among non-influential
genes; the HHI edge of the mechanism is mapped at one point, not traced. Model outputs in
the package are released for research documentation; licensing of third-party model
outputs is flagged in the dataset card.
